What the service does

The Social Publishing Platform lets the members of a workspace connect social media accounts through each network’s official OAuth flow, and create, schedule, and publish content through those networks’ official APIs. You can disconnect or revoke those connections. We never ask for or store your social network passwords.

Data we process

  • Your account: email address, optional display name, and a password stored only as a salted hash (scrypt).
  • Sign-in sessions: a hash of the session token, the IP address and browser (user agent) used, and dates. They are deleted when the session expires or you sign out.
  • Password reset: if you request a reset, a one-time token is stored until it expires or is used. The new password is stored only as a hash.
  • Workspaces: name, time zone, members, and their roles.
  • Invitations: the email address and offered role of a person invited to a workspace.
  • Brands or clients: names and workspace settings you create to organize accounts and content.
  • Connected social accounts: the identifiers the network gives us (for Instagram, your Instagram user ID and the ID that is specific to our app), name, username, profile picture URL, account type, and the permissions you granted.
  • Authorizations (tokens): the access tokens issued by each network. They are encrypted with AES-256-GCM (envelope encryption) before being stored, are never shown or sent to your browser, and are erased when you disconnect the account or request deletion.
  • Content and publishing: the text, schedule, target accounts, and attached media references of each post you create, and the result returned by the network (post ID and URL).
  • Media library: if you upload files, the service stores metadata (original name, type, size, dimensions, duration, tags, folder, and alt text). The file itself is stored in object storage configured for the service, not in the application database. Preview access uses time-limited signed URLs. Production storage provider: [PENDING: production storage provider].
  • Notifications from networks: events that Meta sends to our webhook endpoints (for example, that an authorization was revoked).
  • In-app notifications: alerts about connections and publishing status for workspace members.
  • Audit log: security-relevant actions (connections, disconnections, deletions) with internal identifiers, dates, the network involved, and the permissions granted. It contains no tokens, emails, names, usernames, or network identifiers.
  • Data deletion requests: confirmation code, status, dates, a count of what was deleted, and a one-way hash of the network identifier so repeated requests can be recognized.

Instagram

When you connect an Instagram professional account we request instagram_business_basic (to read your account’s ID, username, and profile so we can show which account is connected) and instagram_business_content_publish (to publish the content you create or schedule). We use Instagram data only to provide these features to you.

Use of information received from Meta APIs (Facebook, Instagram, Threads) complies with the Meta Platform Terms and Developer Policies.

Why we use it

Only to provide the service you request: connecting your accounts, publishing and scheduling on your behalf, showing you the status of each connection and post, warning you when a connection needs attention, inviting collaborators you choose, keeping the service secure, and complying with legal obligations. We do not sell your data and we do not use it for advertising.

How we protect it

The following controls are implemented in the application:

  • Application traffic is served over HTTPS in production.
  • Social network tokens are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM before storage.
  • Passwords are stored only as salted scrypt hashes, never in clear text.
  • Access tokens are not returned to the browser or written to application logs.
  • Each workspace’s data is isolated from other workspaces; only that workspace’s members can access it, according to their role.
  • We do not store social network passwords.

This policy does not claim certifications, audits, or other security practices that are not implemented.

How long we keep it

  • Account, workspace, media, and content data: while your account or workspace exists, until you delete it.
  • Tokens: until you disconnect the account, the network revokes the authorization, or you request deletion.
  • Expired or closed sessions, and unused password-reset tokens: deleted automatically when they expire or are used.

How to request deletion

You can delete your data in any of these ways:

  • Disconnect a social account in the application. Tokens for that account are erased, scheduled posts for that account are cancelled, and the account stops being available for publishing.
  • From Instagram or Facebook: go to Settings ? Apps and websites, remove the Social Publishing Platform, and request deletion of your data. Meta notifies the application. Removing the app without requesting deletion only revokes the authorization: we stop using the tokens and pause scheduled posts for that account.
  • Delete a workspace or your account in the application. A workspace owner can delete the whole workspace. Any person can delete their own account. If you are the only owner of a workspace that still has other members, transfer ownership or delete the workspace first.
  • By email: write to ssseleniasanchez@gmail.com from the email address of your account, and ask for your data to be deleted. You can also use the contact form on iamselenia.com.

When a deletion request is processed, the application deletes:

  • Tokens of the affected connections (the stored encrypted values are erased).
  • Connected social accounts and data derived from them: profile, permissions, authorizations, scheduled and past publications to those accounts with their results, and related notifications.
  • Notifications sent by Meta that mention the person.
  • If you delete a workspace: its content, posts, members, connections, and media files.
  • If you delete your account: your sessions, memberships, the connections you made, and workspaces in which you were the only member. If you created content in a workspace that other people still use, that content stays in that workspace and your account is anonymized (email, name, and password are erased).

What is kept:

  • Content already published on a social network is not deleted from that network. You can delete it in the network itself.
  • The audit log and the record of the deletion request (confirmation code, status, dates, and counts), without tokens or network identifiers.
  • Database backups until they expire ([PENDING: backup retention in days]) and application logs until they expire ([PENDING: log retention in days]).

Instagram does not offer an API to revoke an authorization from our side; we erase our tokens, and you can also remove the app from your Instagram settings.
